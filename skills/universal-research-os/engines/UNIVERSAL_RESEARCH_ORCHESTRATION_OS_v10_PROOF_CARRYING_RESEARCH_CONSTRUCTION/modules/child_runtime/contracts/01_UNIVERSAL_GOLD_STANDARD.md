# Universal Gold Standard

## 1. Topic correctness
A child session must research only its assigned topic. If the topic brief is unreadable, it stops instead of inventing a topic.

## 2. Content before process
Every child pack must specify:
- mission
- research modules
- canonical ownership
- explicit exclusions
- core questions
- expected nodes
- mandatory source seeds
- debates and failures
- evidence-backed case types
- topic-specific semantic risks

A process-only package is invalid.

## 3. Verified sources
Accepted sources require verified metadata and honest access level. Search result pages, incorrect DOI mappings and unverifiable records cannot support production claims.

## 4. Atomic claims
Claims must be small enough that each source relation can be evaluated. Multi-clause synthesis is split or labelled as synthesis.

## 5. Semantic provenance
Relationships:
- support
- partial-support
- contextual-support
- challenge

Structural linkage alone is not enough. The source must actually support the stated part.

## 6. Independent evidence
Multiple websites repeating one original source count as one evidence family.

## 7. Source-note anti-overattribution
Every source note states:
- what it establishes
- what it contributes partially/contextually
- what it does not establish
- caveats and transfer limits

## 8. Cases
Evidence-backed cases and illustrative scenarios are separated. Illustrative examples do not count toward the case floor.

## 9. Alias purity
Aliases contain genuine synonyms, translations, abbreviations or historic names only. They cannot contain benchmark questions or scenario sentences.

## 10. Reproducible QA
Final claims of PASS require executable scripts, hashes, ranked outputs and machine-readable validator results.

## 11. Zero unresolved warning rule
The package cannot call itself production-ready while validators have unexplained warnings.

## 12. Reconciliation
Child packages are merge candidates, not the final global knowledge base. The master dispatcher must reconcile IDs, ownership, overlap, sources, conflicts and cross-links.
